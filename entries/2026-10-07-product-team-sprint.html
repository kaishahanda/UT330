Reflecting on the Product Team Sprint

October 7, 2026

Home Log Project About

Working on the Product Team Sprint was an experience where I had to be intentional and think more thoughtfully about my own experiences when interacting with platforms. When interfaces work well to address a problem or provide a specific service, it often goes unnoticed and unrecognized by me and so many others because of how effortless it seems. Yet, in this assignment, it was imperative that the features we know work well and are effective were upheld and integrated into the interface we were developing.

One of the most notable pieces of feedback I received while drafting this mockup was the importance of providing less information and understanding the impacts of information fatigue. "Less is more" has always been an important principle in design, and while designing the "Duplicate Report Page," I realized that explaining information concisely and redirecting the user would be more useful than having an extensive amount of information on the page. This made the page more digestible and allowed it to mirror an interface that does not require much mental effort to navigate.

I also really appreciated the level of cohesiveness and connectivity that must go into designing an interface that spans numerous screens. Ensuring that there is a navigation bar on every page, as well as buttons on screens that redirect users to the next step in the process, is important for any user. The features that anticipate a user's next move are what truly make an interface work so effortlessly.

Working with the group was also a great experience. There were many opinions and strategies shared throughout the process, yet that is one of the reasons we were able to cultivate such a thoughtful and intricate interface. One of the challenges I experienced was working virtually. Although it was very convenient for everyone to work collaboratively while also getting a couple of extra hours of sleep, I found that I sometimes struggled with staying engaged. Being in an environment with a sense of camaraderie and productivity helps me personally stay engaged and involved in the collaboration. Regardless, the collaboration was there; it would have just been heightened for me if we had worked in person.

Kaisha Handa. UT330, Taubman College, Fall 2026.
